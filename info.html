🚨 If you have an accident

  1. Stop and stay calm. Switch on the hazard lights and put out the warning triangles behind the vehicle.
  2. Check for injured people. Call 10111 or the MVA Fund on 9682 if anyone is hurt. Help only as far as it is safe.
  3. Do not move the vehicle unless a police officer allows it, or it blocks the road. If you must move it, first mark where it stood.
  4. Take lots of photos: all vehicles, the damage, number plates, the road, and the scene from several sides.
  5. Get the other driver’s details: name, phone, ID or passport number, driving licence number, registration and a description of their vehicle.
  6. Call Go Rent on +264 81 861 8085 straight away, before anything is repaired or towed.
  7. Report it to the police within 24 hours at any police station, and ask for the case number. This is the law in Namibia. Without a police report no insurance claim can be made.
Hit an animal, rolled over or went off the road?Call us and report it to the police the same way, even if nobody else was involved.

🔧 Breakdown or flat tyre

  • Call Go Rent first (+264 81 861 8085). Do not let anyone repair the vehicle without our OK. Unauthorised repairs are for your own cost.
  • Stay with the vehicle. In a national park, never get out. Wait inside and call for help.
  • Your vehicle has 2 spare wheels, a jack and a wheel spanner. Change a wheel only on firm, flat ground, well off the road, with the triangles out.
  • After changing a wheel, check the wheel nuts again after 50 km, and have the flat tyre repaired at the next town (keep the receipt).
  • A warning light on the dashboard? Stop in a safe place and call us before driving on.

🛣️ Driving on gravel roads

🛞 Tyre pressure: keep it at 1.6 bar (cold tyres)We set the tyres to 1.6 bar, measured when the tyres are cold. This is what we highly recommend for Namibian roads. Do not change it, and do not let fuel stations pump your tyres: they often fill them to 2.4 bar, which is dangerous. The vehicle can become unstable on rough roads. If a tyre looks low, WhatsApp us.

Most roads in Namibia are gravel. Most rental accidents here involve one vehicle only: too fast on gravel, the car slides, and it rolls. These accidents are not covered by insurance.

  • Keep to 80 km/h on good gravel, and go slower on loose, corrugated or unfamiliar sections. Speed is behind almost every gravel accident.
  • Slow right down before crests and bends. You can’t see what is over or around them, and the surface is often loosest there.
  • Never brake hard on gravel. To slow down, lift off the accelerator and let the car settle. Sudden braking is what makes the tyres lose grip.
  • Watch for corrugations and sudden soft or sandy patches, especially in riverbeds and dips. They pull at the steering.
  • Keep both hands on the wheel and leave big gaps. Stay in the tyre tracks: the edges and the middle of the road are loose.
  • When a vehicle comes towards you, ease off and move to the far left. It protects your windscreen from flying stones, and the dust hides the road for a while.
  • Check your tyres and both spare wheels every morning before you drive. Punctures are the most common problem on gravel.
  • Take a break every 2 hours. Long straight roads make you tired.
If the back of the car starts to slideDon’t panic and don’t fight it. Take your foot off the accelerator, hold the wheel firmly, keep steering where you want to go, and let the car slow down and settle. Do not brake hard and do not yank the wheel. That is what turns a wobble into a rollover.
Water on the roadDo not drive through flowing rivers or floodwater. Water crossings are not covered by your insurance. After rain, wait: desert floods usually go down within a few hours. If you are unsure, WhatsApp us before you cross.

🦓 Animals & national parks

  • Kudu, oryx, cattle, goats and warthogs cross the road without warning, especially early morning and late afternoon.
  • In parks: 40 km/h (Go Rent limit), stay on marked roads, and never get out of the vehicle except in camps and marked picnic spots.
  • Do not feed animals. Keep your distance, and back away slowly if an animal looks upset.
  • Park gates open at sunrise and close at sunset. Be inside your camp or out of the park before the gate closes.

⛽ Fuel, distances & planning

  • DIESEL ONLY. Wrong fuel is not covered and is very expensive to repair. Tell the attendant “diesel” every time.
  • Fill up at every fuel station you pass, even at half a tank. Stations can be 200–350 km apart, and a small-town pump can be closed or empty.
  • Fuel stations are served by an attendant. A small tip (a few N$) is normal.
  • Fuel stations take cards. Keep about N$5,000 in cash as a backup, enough for one full tank, in case the card machine is down. No need to carry more.
  • Carry extra drinking water: about 5 litres per person, plus snacks, in case of a breakdown or a long wait.
  • Namibia is big: a day can be 4–6 hours of driving between stops. Plan about 60–70 km per hour on gravel, and always arrive before sunset.
  • Don’t over-pack your days. Two nights in one place leaves time if a tyre or the weather slows you down.
  • Download offline maps before you leave. There is no mobile signal for long stretches between towns.
  • Tell your lodge or campsite where you are going next.

🏕️ Your vehicle

  • Drive in normal 2-wheel drive on tar and on normal gravel roads. Use 4×4 High Range only in thick sand. Use Low Range only for very slow off-road work (max 10 km/h).
  • Keep the canopy locked and valuables out of sight when you park. Never leave bags on the seats.
  • The rooftop tent, fridge, stove and equipment were shown to you at handover. Look at your handover document if you forget how something works, or WhatsApp us.
  • No smoking in the vehicle.
